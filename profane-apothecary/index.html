Profane Apothecary

A Pathfinder who swings Marohi Erqi once every 1.85 seconds. Each swing is timed so that it is Ancestrally Boosted, exerted and flask-charged at the same moment, then lands as chaos damage on a cursed boss standing on Profane Ground. I designed it from my own Path of Building runs instead of adapting an existing guide. Nobody has played it yet.

Read this first

This is a theorycraft. The damage and defence numbers are Path of Building output for builds I assembled and optimised myself, on 3.29 game data with the 3.30 Master Distiller values patched in. Several interactions are assumptions I could not test, and they are listed with their cost in Unknowns and risks. Treat Stage 1 as a cheap experiment before you commit currency to the later stages.

The build in seven lines

  • Boss button: Earthshatter of Prominence, Fist of War, Melee Physical Damage, Pulverise, Void Manipulation, Increased Critical Strikes, six-linked in Marohi Erqi.
  • Pack button: Sunder, Faster Attacks, Increased Area of Effect, Fist of War in the body armour.
  • Ascendancy: Nature's Reprisal, Nature's Adrenaline, Master Distiller, Nature's Boon, in that order. After you kill Lycia, trade Nature's Boon for Bitter Heresy.
  • Campaign: Poisonous Concoction from level 12, with the slam gems leveling in your second weapon set. Swap to the maul between level 66 and 68.
  • Flasks: Amethyst and Diamond are batteries you never press. Sin's Rebirth and a Sulphur flask drink themselves. The tincture is a toggle.
  • Tree rule: keep the swing at 1.85 seconds or slower. Take Unwavering Stance and Iron Reflexes. Never add attack speed.
  • Boss rule: totems, mark, Enduring Cry, then hold the slam and stay close until the automatic warcry detonates your spikes.

02How it works

The character exists to land one very large hit and repeat it on a strict beat. Slow attacks are usually a weakness. Here they are the point, because three of the strongest multipliers in the game are rationed by time or by skill use, and a slow swing can claim all of them on every hit.

The 1.85-second rule

Fist of War boosts a slam only if 1.8 seconds have passed since the last boosted one. Path of Building averages this. It assumes you collect one boost every 1.8 seconds however fast you swing, so more attack speed always looks like a small gain.

The game does not average. A boost cannot be saved up. If a swing takes 1.5 seconds, the second slam starts before the timer is ready and gets nothing, and the third is boosted again. Half the boosts are gone.

That gives attack speed a cliff. On the Stage 3 character every slam is boosted at 0.54 attacks per second, for 15.4M. Add 5% attack speed and real damage falls to 12.1M (−21%), while Path of Building's averaging reports a rise to 16.0M. Damage does not climb back to where it started until about +50% attack speed, and a second cliff waits at 1.08 attacks per second.

So the build treats attack speed as a budget. The tree carries exactly enough to reach a 1.85-second swing, which leaves 0.05 seconds of slack for server timing, and every other source is refused: no attack speed on gear or jewels, no Onslaught, no passive that grants speed on full life or after placing a totem. I changed my copy of Path of Building to use the real rule. Because each checkpoint sits on the safe side of the cliff, an unmodified copy shows the same damage.

What nine seconds look like

Earthshatter of Prominence hits once with the fissure (8.6M at Stage 3) and plants one large spike under the target, which is worth 20.0M when it shatters. Spikes shatter when you warcry or use a different slam, not when you use Earthshatter again. The automatic Intimidating Cry does that job, so each cry detonates the two or three spikes planted since the last one. A spike waits six seconds, longer than the gap between cries, so none is lost as long as you are still near it when the cry fires.

Why you cannot be stunned

A 1.85-second swing that gets interrupted is a slam that never happens, and Master Distiller has already spent its flask charges. So the tree takes Unwavering Stance, one point off the path, and Iron Reflexes to turn the evasion on hybrid armour into armour you can still use. On the Stage 3 tree the pair costs 6% of Path of Building's effective health figure compared with keeping evasion, and Path of Building gives stun immunity no value at all. I consider it the most important defensive choice in the build.

The rest of the defence is ordinary and honest: five endurance charges from Enduring Cry (Stamina and Vigour raise the cap), Fortify from the mastery, Arctic Armour because you stand still to swing, Steelskin on Cast when Damage Taken, 65% spell suppression, and 579 life per second from leech while you are hitting, with 236 from regeneration. The life pool is the weak point. Defences measures each of these layers.

Two ideas I did not ship

Twin six-links

The community wiki says each Fist of War gem keeps its own timer. Two identical six-links, one in the maul and one in the body armour, pressed in strict alternation would let a faster character boost every slam. At 0.65 attacks per second that is +21% against this build, before paying for the attack speed.

It costs the pack-clear link and the guard skill, it needs two keys in perfect rhythm, and it rests on a line I could not test. Worth trying as a boss-only setup once the base build is proven.

Three trees, one chosen

I wrote a small optimiser around Path of Building's own node-power calculator. It scores a tree as the log of damage plus the log of effective health, buys the best notable per point, then keeps removing the weakest cluster and refilling.

From several starting points it settled on three trees for Stage 3. Against the one in this guide, the first has 16% more damage, 6% less life and 11% less effective health; the third has 15% less damage, 5% more life and 13% more effective health. I chose the middle one because the same tree gives noticeably sturdier early checkpoints. The first is a five-point swap described under the passive tree.

Getting Earthshatter of Prominence

It is a transfigured gem, so it does not come from a vendor. The Divine Font at the end of a Labyrinth can turn a normal Earthshatter into one of its transfigured versions, but the option is not always offered and the result is random. In practice you buy it from another player. Until then the Sunder five-link above does the same job.

The attack speed budget

At Stage 3 the swing is 1.85 seconds. The tree supplies 27% increased attack speed in total: Destroyer (5%), Art of the Gladiator (10%), Finesse (8%), Born to Fight (4%). The Two Hand mastery removes 10%, and Pulverise and Melee Physical Damage slow the six-link further. That sum is the whole budget.

Things that silently break the build

  • Any attack speed on gloves, rings, jewels, the amulet or an anoint.
  • Onslaught from a flask, a support gem or a shrine. A shrine in a map is harmless; on a boss it halves your boosts until it ends.
  • Passives with conditional speed. The optimiser was barred from Gladiator's Perseverance (full life), Ancestral Echo (after placing a totem), Relentless Pursuit (after casting a mark) and the "per enemy in close range" attack mastery.
  • A low-level Fist of War. Its timer is 1.87 s at gem level 18 and 1.80 s at level 20, so an under-levelled gem needs a slower swing.

If you see the ancestor appear on every second slam instead of every slam, you have picked up speed somewhere.

The damage dial

If you want more damage and accept less life, move five points: refund Heart of Oak with its travel node and the Armour, Leech and Life masteries, then take Disemboweling and the Critical mastery that adds 25% multiplier against unique enemies. Measured at Stage 3 that is about 16% more damage (roughly 18.0M instead of 15.4M), with 5,047 life instead of 5,363 and about 11% less effective health.

Ascendancy, bandits, pantheon

Pathfinder, in order

Nature's Reprisal first: while you level with Poisonous Concoction it is the best node on the wheel, and the finished build wants it anyway. Nature's Adrenaline second, so every flask refills by itself on bosses. Master Distiller third, just before you swap to the maul. Nature's Boon fourth. If you level with slams from level 1, take Master Distiller first instead.

Lycia bloodline

Kill Lycia, Herald of the Scourge, at the end of the Forbidden Sanctum and use the altar she leaves. Bloodline passives spend the same eight ascendancy points, so refund Nature's Boon and its small node and take Bitter Heresy. You can be carried for the kill; your character only has to be present.

Bandits

Kill all three for the passive point. Path of Building counts on it at every checkpoint.

Pantheon

Low stakes. Unwavering Stance already rules out stuns and Arctic Armour already rules out freeze, so the Brine King has little left to give. I would take Soul of Solaris for bosses (physical damage reduction against a lone enemy) and Soul of Lunaris in maps, with Soul of Tukohama as the minor god because you stand still to swing. I have not checked any of them against 3.30 text.

Iron Reflexes makes evasion count

The keystone converts all evasion into armour. Take it out and the Stage 3 character loses 59% of its armour and 16% of its effective HP. Increases to evasion and increases to armour both act on the converted part, which is why pure evasion bases come out level with the hybrids on armour.

Suppression needs a Dexterity base

Spell suppression only rolls on gear with a Dexterity requirement. Pure armour pieces cannot have it, and that is most of the 13% they cost in the table. Farrul's Fur sits on a hybrid base, but it is a unique with fixed modifiers and suppression is not one of them, which is why suppression falls to 50% at Stage 4.

The Armour mastery wants armour on all four

It gives +1% to all maximum elemental resistances while helmet, body armour, gloves and boots each have armour. One pure evasion piece switches it off. That is the 4% on the biggest elemental hit in the table.

What to buy

Hybrids first. A pure evasion piece with suppression is a fair stopgap; the bases in the test need up to 183 Dexterity and the Stage 3 character has 256. Avoid pure armour and anything with energy shield. On each piece look for life, then suppression, then resistances.

08Flasks and the charge engine

Master Distiller does not care whether a flask is running. Each time you use a skill it takes three charges from one flask of each type it knows, and grants that flask's bonus to that one use. So the Amethyst and Diamond flasks are batteries, and the belt's real job is to refill them faster than a slam empties them. A slow swing makes that easy, which is the third reason this build is slow.

Charges in

  • Nature's Adrenaline: every flask gains 3 charges every 3 seconds.
  • Nature's Concoction (from Stage 2): while the tincture in slot 3 is active, the flasks in slots 2 and 4 gain 2 charges when you hit with the maul, once a second at most.
  • Belt and prefixes: increased charges gained on the belt and Perpetual on the two batteries multiply all of it.
  • Kills: in maps, monsters refill everything and the engine never runs dry.

Charges out

  • Every slam: 3 from the Amethyst and 3 from the Diamond, less the belt's reduced charges used.
  • Every other skill you use yourself: Sunder, Frostblink, a totem cast and a manual Enduring Cry all pay the same toll. Keep extra presses rare on bosses.
  • Overflow: a flask enchanted to be used when full drinks itself. That is how the Diamond, Sin's Rebirth and the Sulphur flask run without a key press.

The tincture and your mana

An active tincture stacks Mana Burn on you every 0.7 seconds (0.5 for Oakbranch), and each stack drains 1% of maximum mana per second. The tincture mastery makes the first six stacks free, and Essence Sap's mana leech holds the rest for a while. When mana reaches zero the tincture switches itself off and all tinctures go on an eight-second cooldown. My model puts it at roughly two thirds uptime, and the sustained figures use 65%.

Treat it as a cooldown you own: switch it on when a damage window opens. Your slam costs 33 mana at Stage 3 with the two crafted ring mods, and a few more before that. If the tincture has just emptied you, leech refills that in a moment, but you will feel one swing hesitate.

09Playstyle

Maps

  • Frostblink into a pack and Sunder. The wave travels on its own and every monster it hits sends out a shockwave. One or two swings clear a screen at about one attack per second.
  • For a rare, an essence monster or a map boss, use the boss slam and then one Sunder. Sunder is "another slam", so it detonates the spike at once instead of waiting for the warcry.
  • Kills keep every flask full. In maps all your buffs are simply on.
  • Press Enduring Cry when you enter a dense pack. Five endurance charges are a quarter of your effective health.

Bosses

  1. Place totems twice (three totems), cast Assassin's Mark.
  2. Enduring Cry on top of the boss. From Stage 3 this also lays Profane Ground under both of you.
  3. Switch the tincture on and hold the boss slam.
  4. Roughly every fourth slam, recast totems and Enduring Cry. Each extra press costs both batteries three charges, so do not spam them.
  5. When you must move, move right after a slam lands. The Fist of War timer keeps counting while you walk, so the next slam is still boosted. Moving costs time, never the boost.
  6. Stay near your spikes until you hear the automatic warcry.

What kills this character

  • Committing to a swing under a telegraph. You are rooted for 1.85 seconds. Learn which boss attacks leave room for a swing and which do not. Your largest survivable physical hit is about 16,154 at Stage 3.
  • No life flask. From Stage 2 all five slots are spoken for. Recovery is leech (579 per second while you are hitting), regeneration (236) and the short burst from Enduring Cry. Swap the Sulphur flask for a life flask while you learn a fight; it costs 7% damage and the armour that flask gave.
  • Physical reflect maps. Your slam is chaos only while Sin's Rebirth is running. When it lapses, a 28.6M physical hit comes back at you. Do not run that modifier. Stage 1 has no conversion at all.
  • Modifiers that starve the engine: reduced flask charges gained, and "cannot leech". Extra monster chaos resistance only costs damage.
  • Corrupted blood and shock. From Stage 2 nothing removes corrupted blood, so put "Corrupted Blood cannot be inflicted on you" on a jewel. Nothing softens shock. Freeze is covered: Arctic Armour makes you immune.
  • Chaos damage before Stage 3. Chaos resistance is 61% at Stage 2 and 73% at Stage 3 because the Amethyst flask is never drunk on bosses.

Getting the Lycia bloodline

Lycia waits at the end of the Forbidden Sanctum, and this character is a poor Sanctum runner: it stands still, swings slowly and takes hits, all of which burn resolve. Plan to be carried for the kill or to run the Sanctum with a friend. You only have to be present when she dies and then use the altar. Until then, Stage 2 with Nature's Boon is a complete build.

What was checked, and what was not

  • Names. The game rejects a whole filter over one unknown item name. All 53 class names and 107 base type names in the file, plus the 5 partial names ("Awakened", "Cluster Jewel", "Empower", "Enhance", "Enlighten"), were found in the lists of NeverSink's filter as updated for 3.30 on launch day. I used that filter as a name list only; no rule or colour is taken from it.
  • Syntax. Every line was checked against GGG's item-filter page: known keyword, right kind of value, numbers in range. The item-tier and Fated keywords come from GGG's 3.30 filter post.
  • Behaviour. I wrote a small interpreter of the filter language and ran 119 hand-picked items through the file, each with the result the guide needs. All 119 match. Every block in the file is reached by at least one test item.
  • The build's own gear. All 89 items from the seven import codes, dropped at the area level of their checkpoint, come out visible and marked.
  • Sweep. Every equipment base in Path of Building's data, at each rarity and 19 area levels: 46,562 combinations. No unique is hidden anywhere, no rare is hidden below tier 11 maps, no rare armour and evasion piece or rare jewellery is hidden at all.
  • Not checked. The game's own loader, as said above. The interpreter is my reading of GGG's documentation, so a rule could behave differently in play; the socket-colour rules for the green links are the likeliest place. I have not heard the alert sounds. I do not know what the Fated flag marks, how common tier 4 and 5 items are, or what Viscera sells for.
  • Not a farming filter. It knows what this character wears, not what things sell for. When you start farming for profit, switch to a general filter that is updated with prices each league.
